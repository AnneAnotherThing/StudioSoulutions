All four, up and running

Your questions, and exactly what happens now.

  1. “If a studio wants to add pictures, do we have an edit form?”
    We do now! The join form grew two buttons right at the top: I'm new here and Update my listing. A studio that's already on the app taps the second one, tells us their suite and what they'd like changed, and that's the whole job. They don't start over, and they don't have to remember what they put last time.

    Photos went from three to four, too. The first one they choose becomes their profile shot, the big picture at the top of their card, and the other three sit underneath as a little gallery. So if someone's card looks a bit bare next to the others, this is the fix, and it takes them about a minute standing in their own studio.
  2. “Can we add Instagram, Facebook and website links?”
    Yes, and I threw in TikTok while I was in there. The form now collects Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and a website, and whichever ones a studio gives us turn into neat round icon buttons on their card.

    They only show up if there's actually something to link to, so a studio with just an Instagram gets one clean button instead of three sad empty ones. And they can type it however they like. @yourstudio, or the whole long address pasted straight in, and it sorts itself out either way. That matters more than it sounds, because half of them will paste from their phone.
  3. “Is it possible for them to get a notification that their info is received?”
    They do now, within seconds of hitting send, as long as they left an email address. It's a short, warm note saying we've got it and someone's taking care of it, and it's worded a little differently depending on whether they're brand new or asking for a change.

    Honestly? This one's really a gift to you. It's the difference between a studio wondering whether that form did anything and stopping you in the hallway to ask, and them just knowing.
  4. “Make it notify me differently when a change is requested.”
    Three ways, so it's genuinely impossible to miss:

    The email subject starts with [CHANGE]. That looks boring on purpose. Boring is what sorts and searches properly in every mail app, and a pretty design doesn't. The email itself arrives in a different colour, with what they want changed sitting right at the top rather than buried down among the details. And in the admin panel, change requests get their own section with a button that jumps straight to that studio's listing, so there's no hunting for them.

    When one's dealt with it gets marked done rather than deleted, so months from now we can still answer “hang on, what did she originally ask for?”
If you remember one thing off this whole page: the link in the “something's changed” card below does nearly all of it. It opens the form already flipped to Update my listing, so the studio doesn't have to find anything.

What actually happens when someone fills the form in

Worth knowing, so you can tell a studio with real confidence what to expect, and so it's clear which bits are yours and which are mine.

1. You get the link to them

A text, usually. This is your whole part, and it's the part nobody else can do. They'll fill it in because you asked them to, not because a poster told them to.

2. They fill it in, from their phone

Standing in their own studio, usually, taking the photos right there. Two minutes.

3. They get a note back straight away

Automatic, within seconds. Just enough to say we've got it and someone's on it, so they're not left wondering.

4. It lands with me

New studio or change request, and the two look completely different in my inbox, so a change can't get buried behind a pile of new sign-ups.

5. I build or update the card

Tidy the wording, crop the photos, check the suite against the map, publish. Usually same day.

6. It's live, and this page updates itself

Those counts at the top move on their own. Nothing to refresh, nobody to tell.

So your part is really only ever step one. Everything after that is mine. And if something looks wrong on the app, a typo or a wrong suite or a photo that's gone sideways, please don't wrestle with it. Just tell me and it'll be sorted, usually before you've finished telling me.
